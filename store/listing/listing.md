## Description

Screentime shows where your day goes on your PC.

Your day is drawn on a 24-hour dial, midnight at the top, with every app in its own colour taken from its icon. At a glance you can see what filled each hour: the morning's work, the afternoon's meetings, the evening's games.

Click an app to see exactly when you used it. The dial shows only its slices, and the centre shows its total and its share of your day. The Week view puts the last seven days side by side, with your most used apps.

It counts only real use. When you step away, the time you were idle is taken off (you choose after how long). A locked screen is not counted, and pop-ups and hidden windows are ignored.

It is private by design. Screentime makes no network connections at all: no account, no telemetry, no ads. For each app it stores only its name, its program path and when you used it, in a file on your PC. It never records window titles, web addresses, keystrokes or screenshots.

It is light. A small tracker runs in the background using under 10 MB of memory and close to 0% of your CPU. The window only opens when you want it.

Start at login is on by default so your whole day is counted. You can turn it off in Settings or in Windows Settings > Apps > Startup.

Free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. Open source under the MIT License.

- Export every session to CSV or JSON
- Light and dark themes that follow Windows
- No network connections, no account, no telemetry
- Under 10 MB of memory for the background tracker

## Facts behind the numbers

- Under 10 MB of memory: the background tracker measured under 10 MB working set in both the one-file app and the packaged test install (store/README.md). The listing gives no exact figure.
- ~0% CPU: 0.001% of total CPU over two minutes of normal use on Windows 11.
- No network: the app declares no network capability and contains no networking code.
